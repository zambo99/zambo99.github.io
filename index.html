Professional Summary

I am an Assistant Professor at the Security Group of the Eindhoven University of Technology, in the Netherlands. Together with Luca Allodi, I lead the Security Operations and Cyber Threat Intelligence research lines of the group, and I contribute to its network monitoring research. My research focuses on security operations, network intrusion detection and cyber threat intelligence, with a long-standing interest in the security of Industrial Control Systems (ICS). I study how Security Operations Centers (SOCs) detect and investigate attacks, looking both at the human analysts who triage alerts and at the detection rules that generate them, and I collaborate with commercial SOCs, including the Eindhoven Security Hub SOC.

I was one of the three founders of the spin-off SecurityMatters, with the goal of bringing to the market SilentDefense, a platform for network monitoring, asset inventory and network intrusion detection for Industrial Control Systems. At SecurityMatters I served as the CTO, and I was responsible for the company’s R&D.

Research

My research sits at the intersection of security operations, network intrusion detection and cyber threat intelligence, and is carried out in close collaboration with commercial Security Operations Centers (SOCs), including the Eindhoven Security Hub SOC.

Security operations. I study how SOCs detect and investigate attacks, focusing on the two components that most determine their effectiveness: the human analysts who investigate alerts, and the detection rules that generate those alerts. On the analyst side, I work on structured threat analysis processes and tooling that help Tier 1 analysts contextualize and correctly classify network security events, and on training approaches that prepare new analysts for operational work. On the detection side, I analyze how network intrusion detection rules are written, maintained and tuned in practice: from measuring rule noise over large real-world rulesets and deriving design principles for effective, low-noise rules (implemented in the open-source tool suricata-check), to studying the detection engineering process itself through measurement platforms such as SuriCap.

Cyber threat intelligence. I investigate the cybercriminal ecosystem as a source of threat intelligence. This work asks which underground communities matter for the threat landscape, what attackers actually buy once a criminal market works, and how researchers can discover and collect data from these communities at scale, across forums and Telegram. Economic theory, and in particular information asymmetry and signalling, provides a lens to explain why some criminal markets function and others do not. The ultimate goal is to turn these observations into intelligence that can inform security operations decisions.

Industrial Control System security. Building on my background as co-founder and CTO of SecurityMatters, I continue to work on the monitoring and protection of Operational Technology networks: characterizing real-world ICS and SCADA networks across critical infrastructures, such as gas distribution and power substation networks, designing network intrusion detection approaches tailored to industrial protocols and processes, analyzing ICS-specific malware such as Industroyer and INCONTROLLER, and studying how ICS honeypots are designed, deployed and how attractive they are to attackers, as well as the threat actors that target industrial and building automation environments, for example by fingerprinting the ICS tools that attackers use to interact with honeypots (ICtoolS).

Teaching

Intrusion Detection Laboratory (2IMS40)

The goal of this course is to provide students with a platform to get in-depth, hands-on experience on all three of the building blocks of cyber security monitoring: network-based, host-based and log-based intrusion detection.

To do so, the course adopts a reverse classroom setup: the course starts by providing students with material covering practical and theoretical elements of security monitoring and additional material and pointers covering all three pillars, and their relationship. The students then form groups, and are able to choose one of the building blocks to explore in depth by developing a fully-fledged laboratory activity for the other students of the course to attend. The lab activities require the analysis of realistic threat scenarios and systems, with the goal of developing effective detection techniques accounting for the specificities of the proposed scenario.

The lab activities are run and coordinated, in class, by the very students that developed them. The development and delivery of these lab sessions in class is also the final examination of the course for the group of students handling it.
